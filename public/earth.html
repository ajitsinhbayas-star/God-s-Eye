Live Satellite Tracking, Orbital Trajectories & Earth Observation

Orbital Eye provides high-precision real-time satellite tracking and 3D Earth orbit visualization. Telemetry coordinates are derived from authoritative two-line element sets (TLEs) published by CelesTrak and the 18th Space Defense Squadron, propagated using standard Simplified General Perturbations-4 (SGP4) mathematical models.

Supported Orbital Regimes & Spacecraft

Monitor Low Earth Orbit (LEO), Medium Earth Orbit (MEO), Geostationary Orbit (GEO), and Sun-Synchronous Orbits (SSO). Featured spacecraft include the International Space Station (ISS), Tiangong Space Station, ISRO Cartosat-3 and EOS-04, NavIC (IRNSS) constellation, Hubble Space Telescope, and Starlink megaconstellation batches.

Telemetry Methodology: Live Observations vs. SGP4 Propagation

Calculated satellite positions reflect high-precision SGP4 propagation from recent orbital epochs. Direct live telemetry feeds (e.g. WhereTheISS.at real-time ping and SatNOGS radio transmitter passes) are synchronized continuously to maintain sub-second visual accuracy.

🔬 SCIENTIFIC EXPLAINABILITY & ASTRODYNAMICS GUIDE

1. Propagated SGP4 vs. Direct Measured Downlink

Continuous real-time GPS telemetry from satellites is rarely publicly broadcasted (with the exception of ISS downlinks). Most orbital tracking computes positions by integrating mean Keplerian elements (Two-Line Element sets) using the SGP4 (Simplified General Perturbations) model, which models the Earth's oblateness (J2, J3, J4 gravitational harmonics) and atmospheric drag.

2. Orbital Element Epoch & Freshness

The Epoch is the exact UTC timestamp when radar tracking stations last solved the satellite's orbital elements. SGP4 propagation accuracy is highest within $\pm 3$ days of epoch. Beyond 30 days, unmodeled drag and station-keeping maneuvers cause error growth.

3. Geometric Conjunction vs. Collision Risk

A geometric conjunction simply means that two calculated orbital paths pass within a small geometric distance at a given time. Official collision assessments require full error covariance matrices, hard-body radius estimates, and orbital tracking confidence to compute true collision probability ($P_c$).

4. Optical Twilight & Naked-Eye Visibility

A satellite is only visible to the naked eye from Earth when three conditions are met: (1) the satellite is above the observer's horizon, (2) the satellite is illuminated by sunlight (not inside Earth's umbral shadow), and (3) the observer on the ground is in darkness (civil, nautical, or astronomical twilight / night).

5. Atmospheric Re-entry Uncertainties

Space objects re-entering the atmosphere experience severe hypersonic aerothermal heating and break up at 80–50 km altitude. Because solar activity causes the upper thermosphere to expand or contract unpredictably, re-entry predictions always carry uncertainty windows of hours or days.
